InTheMoney (Adam) — options-education YouTube channel (another useful lens, not Austin's own confirmed rules). Distilled from ~28 of his videos, prioritized by view count.

**Philosophy**
Three buckets only: long-term holds (80-90%+ of portfolio), LEAPS (~10%, cap ~20%), and the wheel — actively avoids day/swing trading, calls that culture a near-certain-loss game. Don't touch single-leg options until an account is large enough that a loss is a rounding error (his floor: ~$1,000-1,500). Dividends are "forced withdrawals you pay taxes on" — value is already priced in and drops by the dividend on ex-div date; prefers reinvesting companies. Skeptical of over-diversifying once you know what you're doing (cites Buffett: "diversification is protection against ignorance") — prefers ~15-20 companies he understands, diversified by strategy (holds/LEAPS/wheel) rather than ticker count. Trade with a written plan and follow it "like a robot"; never panic-sell; never treat trading as primary income.

**Underlying selection**
Trade what you know — retail edge is domain familiarity, not deep modeling. LEAPS/wheel candidates: liquid blue chips (AAPL/MSFT/AMZN/GOOG-type) or liquid ETFs (SPY/QQQ) only, never illiquid names. For CSPs: only an underlying you're happy to own, sized so 100-share collateral is manageable. Buys dips with a defined reason (support bounce, bullish divergence, oversold RSI), not blind knife-catching.

**Option selection mechanics**
Wheel default: 30-45 DTE, ~0.30 delta (~70% OTM probability). LEAPS default: 0.75-0.90 delta (up to 0.95), 1yr3mo+ to expiry, prefers 1.5-2yr, avoids locking capital past ~2yr. Mental model for break-even: stock price + extrinsic value, not strike + premium. Buy options (calls/LEAPS) in low-IV regimes; sell premium (CSP/CC/spreads) in high-IV regimes. IV-crush hedges: spreads (near-neutral vega), deep-ITM instead of ATM/OTM, or long call + VIX put (advanced/imperfect). Liquidity bar: 1,000+ OI, check volume and bid-ask width as % of contract value, check liquidity at deeper strikes too; negotiate near the mid, never pay the ask.

**Covered calls**
Only sell a strike you're fully fine selling shares at. Within a wheel cycle, sell CCs above actual stock cost (not just adjusted cost basis) so you don't eat into premium already banked. In a downturn, don't run CCs across the whole portfolio (risk of calling away right before a bounce) — commit only a slice; goal is to lose less than buy-and-hold and fund dip-buys, not to profit outright.

**Cash-secured puts**
~0.30 delta, 30-45 DTE, only on names you don't mind owning. If it moves against you: roll down/out for a net credit, only if the annualized return on tied-up collateral justifies it — otherwise just accept assignment. Take-profit at 50% of max premium *of the original put*, not the rolled one. Early assignment while merely ITM (pre-expiration) is rare outside illiquid contracts, near-expiry, or dividend dates.

**The Wheel**
Cycle: sell CSP (~0.30∆/30-45 DTE) → roll for credit to dodge assignment → get assigned → sell CC above cost basis (~0.30∆) → roll for credit to dodge assignment → shares called away → repeat ("triple income": put premium + call premium + capital gain). Track adjusted cost basis carefully — his flagged mistake is selling CCs too low and giving back banked premium. Prefers running it in a Roth (taxable-account premiums/short holds get taxed as short-term gains). In a bear market it's a stabilizer (loses less than holding shares, funds dip-buys), not a money machine — don't overallocate to it.

**LEAPS**
Deep-ITM LEAPS (delta ~1) approximate 100 shares with far less buying power tied up; extra risk vs. owning shares outright is just the extrinsic value paid. Cap ~10% of portfolio (max ~20%). On losers: don't reflexively roll down every fixed-% drop (backtested worse through 2022 — "feeding the furnace"); better to hold to ~3 months-to-expiry (theta ramp point), then roll down/out once. Never panic-sell — let it die worthless within your allocation cap, or roll/replace within budget; if you can't afford another LEAP, buy shares instead to average down. Cites a follower turning ~$20k→~$150k on LEAPS in a bull market (at an unrecommended 100% allocation) vs. another losing ~$200k from overallocating and panic-selling.

LEAPS strike selection (ITM vs ATM): ATM strikes have a higher breakeven (more extrinsic value to decay), so the stock must move faster to pay off — but leverage per dollar invested is higher (delta/$ is better even though raw delta is lower). Tradeoff: lower probability of success, since a higher strike is simply higher risk. If not completely confident, lean toward a single LEAP under 10% of portfolio; if it runs ITM, use the profits (mind taxes in a taxable account) to add a second LEAP or buy stock — don't let position size snowball. Either way, pick ~1.5 years to expiration for runway. A lower strike gives a lower breakeven, higher probability of success, and significantly less leverage than two ATM LEAPS — the preferred default, since the future's unknown; two ATM LEAPS pay off huge if the ticker rips again, but bleed fast if it doesn't. If sizing would push way past 10% of the portfolio, use a debit spread instead.

For LEAPS debit spreads, don't go deep ITM on the long leg — a debit spread squashes the breakeven down, so keep the long leg barely ITM, using optionsprofitcalculator as a general guide. Doubly important to check liquidity of both strikes.

Breakeven math: the common rule "breakeven = strike + premium" is misleading. For ITM calls, the better mental model is stock price + extrinsic value — breakeven is really governed by extrinsic value and its decay. For OTM calls (no intrinsic value yet), you first have to reach the strike, then make up the extrinsic value lost.

**Poor Man's Covered Call (PMCC)**
Capital-efficient substitute for a standard covered call: instead of buying 100 shares, buy a deep-ITM long-dated LEAPS call (0.80+ delta) as the stock proxy, then sell shorter-dated calls against it for income — same short-leg mechanics as a regular CC, just collateralized by the LEAP instead of shares. Lets you capture upside participation plus premium income for a fraction of the capital of owning shares outright. Main risk: a sharp drop in the underlying devalues the long LEAPS leg, so it isn't a free lunch versus real share ownership — still needs the same LEAPS position-sizing discipline as a standalone LEAP. Detailed strike/expiration selection for the short leg wasn't recoverable from his other videos; treat this as a lighter-detail section.

**Credit spreads / iron condors**
Bull put spread = sell put + buy further-OTM put as hedge (net credit, bullish/neutral); bear call spread is the mirror. Used as a smaller-capital ladder: debit call spreads if LEAPS are unaffordable, credit spreads if full CSP collateral isn't. Prefers short-dated (~1mo) over long-dated — faster theta, cheaper/easier to roll, easier to hold a near-term thesis. Sell in high IV. Roll out/further-OTM for net credit to dodge assignment, same as the wheel. Max loss on assignment = (strike width × 100) − net credit. Iron condor = put spread + call spread combined, for a range-bound TA thesis (support/resistance channel) — bigger credit/profit zone but lower POP than a single spread. Avoids far-OTM "high probability" spreads with no real thesis ("pennies in front of a steamroller"). Generally prefers the wheel over spreads — simpler single-leg management.

**Risk management**
Assignment risk rises with strike depth-ITM and proximity to expiry; early assignment barely-ITM pre-expiry is rare outside illiquid/near-expiry/dividend-date contracts. Roll for credit only when annualized return justifies it; otherwise take assignment. Standard take-profit: 50% of max premium on short-premium trades. Position sizing is the main anti-panic lever — LEAPS capped ~10-20%, bulk stays long-term holds. Build an emergency fund/non-market income before scaling into options. Avoid "feeding the furnace" (repeatedly averaging into one losing position through a sustained downturn with no cap).

**Technical analysis**
TA for entry timing, not a standalone system — considers pure-TA day trading close to gambling. Trend via higher-highs/higher-lows or lower-highs/lower-lows; support/resistance from repeatedly-tested levels; breakouts confirmed by volume. Indicators for confluence, never alone: RSI (overbought/oversold, bullish/bearish divergence = his most reliable single signal) and MACD/moving-average crossovers (50/200-day golden/death cross for trend). Head-and-shoulders (and inverse) treated as fractal patterns across timeframes, prone to false positives. Matches timeframe to strategy — weekly/daily for LEAPS and long-term entries, no intraday since he doesn't day trade.
